# Oracle Principal Core Infrastructure Engineer — requisition 344037

Research reviewed: November 3, 2026.

## The exact posting

Oracle Careers lists requisition 344037 as **Principal Core Infrastructure Engineer**, an individual-contributor Product and Research role in Nashville, Tennessee, at career level IC4, with a stated 6–10+ years experience range. The posting says the engineer will support the next generation of accelerated computing and AI on OCI, working directly with strategic customers from proof of concept through production. It explicitly spans GPU/HPC architecture, AI and agentic platforms, Terraform/Ansible/Slurm/Kubernetes automation, capacity planning, performance and reliability troubleshooting, technical collateral, customer advocacy, and influence on OCI engineering.

This is a hybrid of senior infrastructure implementation and technical customer engineering. The job description calls for being hands-on while translating customer requirements, risks, and operational evidence into deployable solutions and product feedback. That interpretation is based on the listed responsibilities, not on private information about Oracle’s team.

## Role analysis

The work has four linked dimensions:

1. **Build the workload foundation:** size and deploy GPU/HPC resources, networks, schedulers, storage, and cloud services for AI training, inference, and agentic applications.
2. **Make delivery repeatable:** automate cluster provisioning, software deployment, telemetry, and capacity planning with infrastructure-as-code and operations tooling.
3. **Prove readiness:** validate customer requirements, performance, security, reliability, and cost through POCs, production rollouts, and incident response.
4. **Close the feedback loop:** document reusable architectures and deployment guidance, advise customer and internal stakeholders, and carry customer-impacting gaps back to OCI engineering.

## Oracle and OCI context

Oracle’s current AI infrastructure materials describe an integrated platform for GPU compute, high-throughput storage, and cluster networking. Its public page lists GPU options including H100/H200 and Blackwell systems, bare-metal compute, RDMA over RoCE v2, up to 3,200 Gb/sec cluster-network bandwidth, and 2.5–9.1 microsecond cluster network latency. These are Oracle-published platform specifications, not statements about the candidate’s own work. They explain why this role’s end-to-end systems view matters: a customer’s result depends on the compute, network, storage, orchestration, and operational controls working together.

Oracle’s FY2026 results reported cloud revenue of $33.989B, up 39% year over year, and the company’s FY2027 Q1 release described cloud infrastructure (IaaS) revenue growth of 121% year over year. Oracle also said it delivered 850MW of additional datacenter capacity in that quarter. These company-reported figures show the scale and pace of OCI infrastructure expansion; they are contextual signals, not a prediction of a particular team’s roadmap.
